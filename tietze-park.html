A Neighborhood Park with a Lasting Legacy

Established in 1924, Tietze Park is an 8.2-acre community park that has served generations of East Dallas residents for more than a century. Located along Skillman Avenue in the Lakewood area, the park is widely regarded as one of Dallas’ most beloved neighborhood gathering places. Its combination of recreational amenities, mature trees, historic architecture, and active community involvement has made it one of the city’s most heavily used parks, earning a reputation as one of the most-used parks per square foot in Dallas.

When the City of Dallas acquired the property in 1924, it sat on what was then the northeastern edge of the city. Originally named Keith Park, it was renamed Tietze Park in 1934 to honor W.R. Tietze, who served as Dallas’ Superintendent of Parks from 1896 to 1933. A respected horticulturist known for his work in floriculture, Tietze studied under renowned botanist Henry Shaw, founder of the Missouri Botanical Garden, and helped shape Dallas’ early park system through decades of dedicated service.

Recreation in the Heart of East Dallas

Over the years, Tietze Park has evolved into a vibrant destination that offers something for visitors of all ages. Families gather at the playgrounds, athletes compete on baseball and soccer fields, swimmers cool off at the community pool, and neighbors enjoy walking beneath the shade of the park’s towering trees.

Today, the park features a wide variety of recreational amenities, including athletic fields for baseball, soccer, and other organized sports, as well as tennis and basketball courts that serve both neighborhood residents and local leagues. Visitors can enjoy the community swimming pool and aquatic center during the summer months, while families with young children take advantage of multiple playgrounds, including a dedicated toddler play area. A walking trail winds through the park, connecting picnic areas, gathering spaces, and the park’s historic sandstone pavilion. More than 75 mature native trees, including live oaks, cedar elms, cedars, and distinctive “kneeling” bois d’arc trees, provide abundant shade and anchor the park’s historic landscape.

Among the park’s most recognizable landmarks is its sandstone pavilion, constructed in 1934 through the Works Progress Administration (WPA). The pavilion has served as the backdrop for countless neighborhood gatherings, celebrations, and community events for generations, adding to the park’s distinctive historic character.

Preserving a Community Treasure

Like many historic parks, Tietze Park has experienced periods of growth as well as challenges. By the late 1990s, aging infrastructure prompted the Dallas Park and Recreation Department and neighborhood organizations to develop a new master plan for the park. Public meetings brought together residents to identify improvements that would better serve the growing community.

Recognizing the importance of preserving this neighborhood landmark, residents established the Friends of Tietze Park Foundation (FOTPF) in 2002. The nonprofit organization was created to support the park through fundraising, volunteer efforts, and long-term planning while representing the interests of park users throughout the surrounding neighborhoods.

Community support soon produced tangible results. A 2003 city bond program provided funding to restore the historic pavilion, improve lighting, and complete additional park enhancements. Working closely with city leaders, neighborhood residents helped shape a revised master plan that included new playgrounds, landscaped gathering spaces, improved pedestrian connections, and upgraded public amenities.

The first phase of improvements was completed in 2007, bringing restored stonework to the historic pavilion, a new roof, upgraded lighting throughout the park, enhanced landscaping, additional seating, and decorative fencing around the pool. That same year, the Lindsey Brown Toddler Playground was dedicated in memory of Lindsey Stephenson Brown, whose family partnered with the Friends of Tietze Park Foundation to create a lasting tribute to her love of the park.

Restoring the Landscape for the Future

Tietze Park’s stewardship has expanded beyond preserving its historic structures and recreational amenities to include active restoration of its natural landscape. In the spring of 2024, the park received a $36,000 Lowe’s Hometowns Grant to establish a new pollinator garden featuring native plantings, accessible pathways, and educational signage. The garden was designed as a certified Monarch Waystation, creating habitat for monarch butterflies and other pollinators while providing opportunities for visitors to learn about native plants and local ecology.

The park’s mature canopy is also being strengthened through the Centennial Tree Project, funded in late 2023. The initiative added 23 new native trees, including American elm, sycamore, bur oak, chinquapin oak, dogwood, and pecan, helping replenish the park’s tree canopy and improve its ecological integrity.

Together, these projects demonstrate Tietze Park’s ongoing commitment to natural restoration and community stewardship. By pairing the preservation of historic features with native vegetation, pollinator habitat, new trees, educational opportunities, and volunteer engagement, the park continues to evolve while maintaining the character that has defined it for more than a century.

A Park Strengthened by Community

Tietze Park’s history reflects the power of community involvement. During periods of economic uncertainty, neighborhood volunteers have consistently stepped forward to help maintain the park, organize cleanup efforts, and advocate for its future.

One notable example came in 2010, when significant budget cuts threatened to close several Dallas public swimming pools, including the one at Tietze Park. Residents, the Friends of Tietze Park Foundation, and park supporters rallied in support of maintaining park services. Their advocacy helped preserve the pool and ongoing maintenance, ensuring the park remained a vital resource for the surrounding community.

Additional improvements continued with the completion of the second phase of the master plan in 2011, including the addition of a walking trail and shaded structures near the playground. These enhancements expanded opportunities for recreation while making the park more comfortable during Dallas’ warm summer months.

Today, Tietze Park remains one of Dallas’ most cherished neighborhood parks. Its blend of historic character, recreational opportunities, mature native tree canopy, pollinator habitat, and strong community stewardship continues to make it a welcoming destination for families, athletes, neighbors, and visitors alike. More than a century after its establishment, Tietze Park stands as a lasting example of how parks bring communities together, preserve connections to the past, and create healthier, more resilient landscapes for the future.
