Established in 1949, Hattie R. Moore Park is a 5.4-acre community park located in the Los Altos neighborhood of West Dallas. While modest in size, the park carries a powerful legacy rooted in service, community advocacy, and the belief that recreational spaces can transform neighborhoods.

Named in honor of Hattie Rankin Moore, a respected West Dallas Methodist minister and community leader, the park reflects her lifelong dedication to creating opportunities for residents and improving the quality of life in her community. More than 75 years after its establishment, Hattie R. Moore Park continues to represent the importance of neighborhood gathering spaces and the lasting impact of individuals who advocate for positive change.

Hattie Rankin Moore dedicated her life to serving the people of West Dallas, particularly those who were often overlooked or excluded. As a Methodist minister, she founded the Eagle Ford Mission, welcoming individuals who had been ostracized by other churches and creating a place where everyone could find community and support.

Her work extended far beyond the walls of the mission. In recognition of her contributions, Eagle Ford Mission was later renamed Rankin Chapel in her honor and eventually became the Wesley Rankin Community Center, continuing the tradition of service and outreach that she established.

One of Rankin’s greatest priorities was addressing challenges facing West Dallas families, especially the lack of safe spaces and opportunities for children. She believed recreation, education, and community resources were essential tools for helping young people build brighter futures.

In a letter to the editor of a local newspaper, Rankin highlighted the urgent need for investment in West Dallas neighborhoods, stating: “Folks wonder why so many West Dallas boys turn out to be criminals. … they haven’t a dog’s chance to be anything else. We have no parks, no playgrounds, no handy schools, no lights, no water, no gas. The dogs in Dallas are housed better than our boys and girls.”

Her words reflected a deep understanding of the connection between community resources and opportunity. For Rankin, parks and recreation were not simply amenities—they were essential foundations for stronger, safer neighborhoods.

Hattie R. Moore Park was created in response to the need for recreational spaces in West Dallas. Located just south of Anita Martinez Recreation Center and Lorenzo de Zavala Elementary School, the park has long served as a neighborhood gathering place for residents of all ages.

The park’s history reflects the continued efforts of the West Dallas community to create spaces where children can play, families can gather, and neighbors can connect. Decades after Rankin advocated for greater access to recreation, the park remains part of the ongoing effort to provide welcoming, safe, and engaging public spaces.

After a long pause caused by the pandemic, plans to renovate and improve Hattie R. Moore Park resumed. Based on input gathered from residents in early 2020, proposed improvements include colorful murals, artificial turf, family grills, updated playground equipment, and additional athletic fields.

These enhancements are designed to build upon the park’s original purpose while creating a more vibrant and enjoyable space for today’s community.

The story of Hattie R. Moore Park is a reminder that parks are more than physical spaces—they are reflections of the communities they serve. The park honors a woman who understood that providing children and families with opportunities for recreation, connection, and support could create lasting positive change.

Nearly eight decades after Hattie Rankin Moore called attention to the need for more resources in West Dallas, the community continues working toward the same goals: safer neighborhoods, stronger connections, and spaces where residents can thrive.

Through continued investment and community involvement, Hattie R. Moore Park carries forward Rankin’s vision. It remains a place where neighbors gather, children play, and the legacy of service and advocacy continues to inspire future generations.
