Honoring a Legacy of Resilience and Community

Established in 1961, Miller Family Park is a 13-acre community park with a powerful history behind it. While today it serves as a neighborhood recreation space with open areas, trails, playgrounds, and gathering places, the land carries a story that reaches back more than 150 years. The park sits on property once connected to a farming community established by formerly enslaved people after the Civil War, making it a significant site that reflects Dallas’s complex history of perseverance, family, and community building.

The park’s modest network of trails winds beneath a mature native tree canopy, providing shade and a natural respite in the heart of the neighborhood. Its riparian setting supports tree species typical of North Texas creek corridors, including pecan, elm, cottonwood, and various oaks. Together, these trees provide ecological continuity with the region’s original landscape while creating a comfortable, welcoming environment for visitors.

The journey of Miller Family Park is deeply connected to the Miller family and the broader history of Oak Cliff. In the mid-19th century, pioneer William Brown Miller arrived in the Dallas area in 1849 and established a large cotton plantation and ferry crossing along the Trinity River near present-day Oak Cliff. His plantation eventually covered approximately 7,500 acres and became one of the area’s most prominent early settlements.

Between 1855 and 1862, Miller used enslaved labor to construct Millermore, a two-story Greek Revival mansion that became the centerpiece of his plantation. Today, Millermore is recognized as the only surviving antebellum plantation home of its kind in Dallas County. In the 1960s, the historic structure was saved from demolition, carefully dismantled, and relocated to Old City Park, where it became an important historic landmark.

From Freedmen’s Land to a Farming Community

Following emancipation, the land’s story continued through the efforts of formerly enslaved people who created new opportunities for themselves and future generations. The area where Miller Family Park now stands was once part of approximately 600 acres purchased by six freed slaves who established a farming community after gaining their freedom.

Among those settlers was John Miller, a freedman who purchased a portion of the land in 1874 and helped establish a thriving agricultural community. Many members of this community adopted the Miller name, creating a lasting connection between the land and the families who worked to build new lives after slavery.

Over time, maintaining ownership of the land became increasingly difficult. Some portions were sold to cover taxes and other expenses, gradually reducing the size of the original property. Despite these challenges, the remaining land continued to represent the determination and contributions of the Miller descendants and the broader freedmen community.

Approximately 50 years ago, the City of Dallas purchased the remaining portion of the property and transformed it into a public park. Originally named Cedardale Park, the site was later renamed Miller Family Park to recognize the historic families whose labor, resilience, and dedication shaped the land.

A Living Landscape Beneath the Canopy

The natural character of Miller Family Park adds another layer to its story. Mature native trees line the park’s trails and open spaces, creating a shaded landscape that reflects the ecological character of North Texas creek corridors. Pecan, elm, cottonwood, and various oak species provide canopy, habitat, and a sense of connection to the landscape that existed before the surrounding neighborhood developed.

Recent “Branching Out” volunteer efforts have built on that legacy by transplanting additional native Texas trees throughout the park. These plantings reinforce the existing flora, strengthen habitat value, and expand the shaded canopy available to park visitors. The effort demonstrates how community stewardship can help preserve and restore the natural qualities of an urban park for future generations.

A Neighborhood Park for Future Generations

Today, Miller Family Park serves as a gathering place for residents of the surrounding community, providing opportunities for recreation, relaxation, and connection. Its trails, mature canopy, open spaces, and recreational amenities create a neighborhood destination where people can enjoy both the natural landscape and the park’s history.

Recent improvements have focused on expanding recreational opportunities for families. Community members, Dallas City Council representatives, and Dallas Park and Recreation Department officials gathered for the groundbreaking of a new playground at the park. The updated amenities include two playgrounds, a swing set, drinking fountain, benches, picnic tables, and new trash receptacles, creating a welcoming environment for children and families.

These improvements help ensure that Miller Family Park remains an active community destination while preserving the historical significance and natural character of the site. The park represents more than just open space. It is a place where residents can gather beneath a growing native canopy while remembering the generations who came before them.

Preserving the Miller Family Legacy

The history surrounding Miller Family Park extends beyond its boundaries. Nearby on Bonnie View Road is the Miller Family Cemetery, a fenced historic site recognized as a Historic Texas Cemetery. The cemetery serves as the final resting place for members of the Miller family, as well as individuals connected to the plantation era, including enslaved people who helped build the Miller estate.

Together, the park and surrounding historic sites tell a story of transformation, from a plantation landscape shaped by enslaved labor to a community space shaped by recreation, family, natural stewardship, and remembrance. Miller Family Park honors both the hardships and achievements of those who shaped Dallas’s past while nurturing the landscape for generations to come.
