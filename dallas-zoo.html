More Than a Century of Wildlife, Conservation, and Discovery

For more than 135 years, the Dallas Zoo has inspired curiosity, education, and conservation while connecting generations of visitors with wildlife from around the world. Spanning 106 acres, the zoo is the oldest and largest zoological park in Texas, welcoming millions of guests to experience animals from the African savanna, South American rain forests, the Australian Outback, and beyond.

Home to more than 2,000 animals representing hundreds of species, the Dallas Zoo has grown from a modest collection of four animals into one of the nation’s premier zoological institutions. Throughout its history, the zoo has continually evolved to reflect changing understandings of animal care, habitat design, conservation, and environmental stewardship while remaining one of Dallas’ most treasured attractions.

Humble Beginnings and a Growing Collection

The Dallas Zoo traces its origins to 1888, when the Dallas City Council purchased two deer and two mountain lions from a private seller in Colorado City, Colorado, for just $60. These first animals were displayed in City Park, marking the beginning of what would become a world-class zoological institution.

As public interest grew, the city expanded the animal collection throughout the 1890s, adding new species and officially supporting the zoo’s continued development. By 1910, the growing collection had outgrown City Park and was relocated to Fair Park before moving permanently in 1912 to a 36-acre site in Marsalis Park in the Oak Cliff neighborhood. The city had purchased the land just a few years earlier, creating a permanent home where the zoo could continue expanding.

Under the leadership of Zoo Commissioner William H. Atwell, the collection grew rapidly with the addition of new exhibits and animals from around the world. During the 1920s, the creation of a dedicated Zoo Commission further strengthened the zoo’s mission. The collection expanded significantly through contributions from famed adventurer, trapper, and animal collector Frank Buck, whose “Bring ’Em Back Alive” expeditions introduced many exotic species to American zoos.

Even during the hardships of the Great Depression, investment in the zoo continued. Throughout the 1930s, the federal Works Progress Administration (WPA) funded major improvements, constructing new buildings and modernizing facilities that helped shape the zoo for decades to come.

Growing into a World-Class Destination

By the mid-20th century, the Dallas Zoo had become one of the city’s most popular attractions. The formation of the Dallas Zoological Society in 1955 provided an important private partner dedicated to supporting exhibits, educational programming, conservation initiatives, and future expansion.

During the 1960s, the zoo displayed more than 500 species and attracted visitors from across Texas. As zoological science evolved in the following decades, the zoo embraced a new philosophy that emphasized conservation, scientific research, animal welfare, and habitat preservation rather than simply displaying animals for entertainment.

The zoo earned accreditation from the Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA) in 1985, reflecting its commitment to the highest standards of animal care and conservation. Around the same time, Zoo Director Warren J. Iliff envisioned an ambitious expansion known as the Wilds of Africa. Designed by architect Herbert W. Reimer, the project introduced naturalistic habitats organized by geographic region, allowing visitors to experience African wildlife in environments that more closely resemble their native ecosystems.

Supported by voter-approved bond measures totaling more than $30 million, the Wilds of Africa opened in 1990, dramatically expanding the zoo and transforming the visitor experience.

Exploring Wildlife Around the World

Today, the Dallas Zoo is divided into two primary areas: ZooNorth, the historic heart of the zoo, and the Wilds of Africa, an immersive expansion that showcases some of the world’s most iconic wildlife.

ZooNorth offers a variety of exhibits that delight visitors of all ages. Families can explore the Lacerte Family Children’s Zoo, visit Primate Place, discover reptiles and amphibians in the Herpetarium, observe native predators in Texas Cats, admire rare wildlife in the ExxonMobil Endangered Tiger Habitat, experience colorful birds at Wings of Wonder, follow the interactive Snout Route, and encounter Australian wildlife at the popular Koala Walkabout.

Across the tunnel beneath Clarendon Drive, visitors enter the Wilds of Africa, where expansive habitats recreate the landscapes of the African continent. Guests can walk the Gorilla Trail, encounter elephants and giraffes in Giants of the Savanna, or board the Monorail Safari for a guided 20-minute journey through six African habitats. The safari experience offers unique views of animals living together in large, naturalistic environments that encourage behaviors similar to those seen in the wild.

One of the zoo’s most celebrated achievements came in 2010 with the opening of Giants of the Savanna. This groundbreaking $32.5 million habitat became the first exhibit in North America—and one of the first in the world—to combine multiple large African species within a shared ecosystem. In 2011, the Association of Zoos and Aquariums recognized the exhibit for its innovative design and commitment to animal welfare.

Inspiring Conservation for Future Generations

The Dallas Zoo has continued to evolve through partnerships, innovation, and community support. In 2009, daily operations transitioned to Dallas Zoo Management, Inc., creating a successful public-private partnership that has allowed the zoo to expand educational programming, conservation efforts, and guest experiences while continuing to serve as a treasured Dallas destination.

Today, every visit offers more than an opportunity to see remarkable animals. Guests learn about biodiversity, endangered species, habitat protection, and the importance of conservation around the globe. Through immersive exhibits, educational programs, and hands-on experiences, the Dallas Zoo encourages visitors of all ages to become stewards of the natural world.

From its humble beginnings with four animals in City Park to its role as a leading center for wildlife conservation and education, the Dallas Zoo remains one of Dallas Park and Recreation’s greatest success stories—a place where discovery, learning, and a love of nature continue to inspire new generations.
