150 Years of Setting the Standard for Parks and Recreation in America

For 150 years, the Dallas Park and Recreation Department (DPARD) has been creating spaces, experiences, and opportunities that bring people together. From beloved parks and recreation centers to historic landmarks and community programs, DPARD’s legacy is built on a commitment to enriching lives, strengthening neighborhoods, and connecting generations.

Explore the People, Places, and Stories of DPARD

The DPARD 150 celebration honors this incredible milestone by inviting the community to explore the people, places, and stories that have shaped Dallas.

Through interactive digital experiences, featured locations, historic figures, audio tours, commemorative merchandise, and community storytelling, residents and visitors can discover the history behind the parks and programs that make Dallas unique.

Celebrating Our History & Building Our Future

This anniversary is more than a look back in time. It’s a celebration of what we’ve accomplished together and a vision for our future. DPARD continues to set the standard for urban parks and recreation by embracing progress, innovation, sustainability, accessibility, and equity.

Join us as we celebrate 150 years of memories made, communities strengthened, and a future built on creating even greater experiences for all.

A Message From John H. Slate, City Archivist

The Dallas Park and Recreation Department (DPARD) celebrates its sesquicentennial in 2026, commemorating the acquisition of the first public park in Dallas in 1876, City Park. In its 150-year history, DPARD has grown from humble origins to become one of the most respected and admired metropolitan park systems in the United States. It has won awards over many years for both its leadership and its 1,600+ dedicated, hard-working staff members. Like many things, DPARD is much more than the sum of its parts.

Parks Are HISTORY

What’s your favorite memory of a Dallas park? Did you have a birthday at a park? Did you have your first kiss in a park? Did you play a game or attend an event at a park that remains in your memory? Hopefully, most of your experiences at parks are good ones—the kind of memories you’ll want to pass down to your own family and future generations of Dallasites.

Dallas parks are both personal history and public history. Our city’s history is tied up with parks. From the exuberance of Juneteenth celebrations and the State Fair of Texas to the tragic assassination of President John F. Kennedy, many important episodes in our park system have figured in local, state, and national history. Some of our park history, such as the era of segregated facilities, serves to remind us that we can overcome obstacles to become an oasis of fairness and equality in public institutions.

Parks Are YOU

Every citizen of Dallas, through municipal taxes, contributes to the design, construction, use, and maintenance of Dallas parks. We use Dallas parks for exercise and fitness, mental well-being, and observing wildlife and plant life. Parks provide peace and a place for reflection. We go to parks to watch the sun rise, and we go to parks to watch the sun set. Dallas parks imbue Dallasites with the good feelings of ownership—that we, collectively, own and enjoy parks as a part of our lives as residents of Dallas.
