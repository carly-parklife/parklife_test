A Century of Golf History in Dallas

Established in 1919, Cedar Crest Golf Course is one of Dallas’s most historic public golf courses and a landmark destination for golfers of all skill levels. Located in southern Dallas, the course has played an important role in the city’s recreational history for more than a century.

From its early years as a premier private golf destination to its current role as a public course serving the Dallas community, Cedar Crest has been shaped by legendary designers, historic tournaments, and generations of players. Its legacy reflects the evolution of golf in Dallas and the importance of recreational spaces for the community.

A Historic Course Designed by A.W. Tillinghast

Cedar Crest Golf Course was designed by renowned golf course architect A.W. Tillinghast, whose work helped define many of America’s most celebrated golf courses during the early 20th century. Known for creating courses that challenged experienced players while remaining enjoyable for everyday golfers, Tillinghast brought his expertise to Cedar Crest, creating a layout that would become one of the region’s most notable golf destinations.

Shortly after opening, Cedar Crest gained national recognition when it hosted the Dallas Open in 1926. The tournament attracted some of the country’s top golfers, with Macdonald Smith earning the victory and adding to the course’s growing reputation.

The following year, Cedar Crest reached an even greater milestone when it hosted the tenth PGA Championship in 1927. The championship brought national attention to Dallas and Cedar Crest, with legendary golfer Walter Hagen claiming the title. Hosting one of golf’s major championships cemented Cedar Crest’s place in the history of the sport and established the course as a significant venue for competitive golf.

From Private Club to Public Community Resource

Cedar Crest’s early history was closely tied to its role as a private country club. However, the country club closed in 1929, leading to a period of transition and changes in ownership. In 1946, the City of Dallas purchased the property, transforming Cedar Crest into a public golf course and expanding access to this historic facility.

The transition to a public course reflected Dallas’s broader commitment to providing recreational opportunities for residents. Cedar Crest became a place where golfers from across the city could enjoy the game, regardless of background or experience level.

The course also played an important role during a significant period in golf history. In 1954, Cedar Crest hosted the United Golf Association (UGA) Negro National Open, a major tournament that provided opportunities for Black golfers during an era when many professional golf events remained segregated. Later that same year, the course hosted the United States Golf Association’s Public Links Championship, further adding to its legacy as a venue for important competitions.

Preserving a Legacy for Future Generations

Over the decades, Cedar Crest Golf Course has continued to evolve while honoring its historic roots. In 2001, a new $2 million clubhouse was constructed, providing modern amenities and improving the experience for golfers and visitors.

In 2004, the course underwent a major renovation led by golf professionals and designers D.A. Weibring and Steve Wolfard. The improvements helped preserve the integrity of Tillinghast’s original vision while updating the course to meet the needs of modern players.

Today, Cedar Crest remains a treasured part of Dallas’s park and recreation system. Its historic fairways have welcomed professional golfers, community players, and generations of families who have enjoyed the game in a beautiful setting.

More than 100 years after its establishment, Cedar Crest Golf Course continues to represent the enduring value of public recreation. Through its championship history, architectural significance, and commitment to serving the community, Cedar Crest stands as a lasting symbol of Dallas’s rich sporting heritage.
