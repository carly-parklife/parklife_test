A Playground Built for Dallas’s Working Families

Fretz Park traces its origins to Trinity Play Park, Dallas’s first municipal playground, established in 1909 in the heart of the city’s Cotton Mills District. Created to serve the children of mill workers, the park provided a safe, supervised place where young people could learn, play, and grow while their parents worked long hours in nearby factories. At a time when organized recreation was uncommon, Trinity Play Park represented a forward-thinking investment in the health and well-being of Dallas families.

The park’s creation was driven by the Dallas Federation of Women’s Clubs, whose playground committee was first led by Olivia Allen Dealey. Through a citywide fundraising campaign known as “Tag Day” in 1908, volunteers sold tags bearing the slogan, “To Help the Children of Dallas, I’m Tagged.” Their efforts, combined with additional fundraising events and growing public support, convinced the city to purchase land at the corner of Corinth Street and Cockrell Avenue. What began as a modest playground eventually expanded to approximately 4½ acres, becoming a model for community recreation.

More Than a Playground

From its earliest days, Trinity Play Park served as much more than a place to play. The park offered an after-school program for children whose parents worked in the nearby cotton mills, along with a kindergarten and community gathering space. Because many homes in the surrounding neighborhood lacked running water, the park also provided public bath facilities, an essential public health service.

The need for these resources was evident in the park’s remarkable attendance. In 1917, Trinity Play Park welcomed 146,493 visitors and provided 10,733 free baths. By 1923, attendance had climbed to 206,090 visitors, while the park continued to offer more than 10,900 free baths. These impressive numbers demonstrate how deeply the park became woven into everyday life for thousands of Dallas residents.

A Center for Health and Community

As the park grew, so did its services. Major improvements arrived with the opening of the Trinity Park Field House in 1915, a state-of-the-art recreation center that included an auditorium, athletic facilities, a motion picture projection room, hot and cold showers, a day nursery, and classrooms for educational programs.

The park also became an important center for children’s health. In 1923, the Dallas Infants’ Welfare and Milk Association established a “pure milk station” within the park. At a time when infant mortality rates were high, the organization distributed safe, pasteurized milk to neighborhood children and provided free milk to families who could not afford it. This simple but vital service helped improve child nutrition and public health in one of Dallas’s working-class neighborhoods.

The park’s impact reached far beyond recreation. In 1923, Dallas Mayor Sawnie R. Aldredge praised Trinity Play Park, writing that it had “been a real service in reducing crime and improving the moral conditions of its neighborhood.” His words reflected the widely held belief that providing children with safe places to play and learn strengthened the entire community.

From Trinity Play Park to Fretz Park

In 1927, Trinity Play Park was renamed Fretz Park to honor Emil Fretz, a longtime member of the Dallas Park Board, upon his retirement. Although the original park served generations of Dallas families, the surrounding Cotton Mills District gradually transitioned to industrial development. In 1964, the original site was closed, and the city exchanged the property to establish the present-day Fretz Park at Hillcrest Road and Belt Line Road in North Dallas.

While today’s Fretz Park occupies a different location, its roots remain firmly planted in the legacy of Trinity Play Park. What began as Dallas’s first municipal playground evolved into a landmark of recreation, education, and public health, and that tradition of serving families continues at the present-day park.

A Modern Park Rooted in Nature

Today, Fretz Park is anchored by the fully renovated Fretz Recreation Center, which offers a gymnasium, meeting rooms, tennis courts, baseball fields, playgrounds, and an outdoor pool known as The Cove, along with a seasonal aquatic center. The Fretz Park Branch Library is also located on-site, extending the park’s longstanding role as a place for recreation, learning, and community connection.

The park’s landscape continues that legacy beneath a rich canopy of native North Texas trees. Cedar elm, bur oak, Shumard oak, live oak, Mexican white oak, chinquapin oak, pecan, American elm, American sycamore, and bois d’arc provide shade and habitat throughout the grounds. Beneath the canopy, redbud, roughleaf dogwood, Mexican plum, fringe tree, wildflowers, and other native plantings add seasonal color and create opportunities for visitors to experience the region’s natural landscape.

In December 2019, a Texas Trees Foundation-led “Branching Out” event brought volunteers together to plant additional native trees along walking paths, sports fields, playgrounds, and picnic areas. The effort strengthened the park’s tree canopy while supporting ecological health, expanding shade, and enhancing the visitor experience.

Today, paved trails connect the park’s recreational spaces while winding through a landscape of mature trees, understory shrubs, and native plantings around the recreation center and library. The result is a park that continues to serve Dallas families in much the same spirit as its predecessor: as a place where recreation, education, health, and community come together, now enriched by a landscape that reflects the native character of North Texas.
