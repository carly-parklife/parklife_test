A Historic Urban Oasis in Dallas

Located approximately five miles northeast of downtown Dallas, White Rock Lake Park is one of the city’s most treasured natural landmarks and one of the most heavily visited parks in the Dallas Park and Recreation system. Spanning more than 1,015 acres, the park provides residents and visitors with a unique opportunity to experience nature, recreation, and history within an urban setting. With miles of trails, open green spaces, wildlife habitats, and scenic views, White Rock Lake remains a beloved destination for walkers, runners, cyclists, bird watchers, paddlers, and families seeking outdoor activities.

The story of White Rock Lake began in the early 20th century when Dallas faced a growing need for a reliable water supply. Construction of the lake began in 1910 as city leaders worked to address water shortages and create a dependable resource for the rapidly growing community. At the time, Dallas Mayor Stephen J. Hay promised residents that the city would develop a water source strong enough to ensure that Dallas would never again face a water crisis. Over time, the lake evolved beyond its original purpose, becoming a defining feature of the city’s landscape and a gathering place for generations of Dallas residents.

Today, White Rock Lake Park is considered one of the largest urban parks in the United States. Its expansive footprint—more than twice the size of New York City’s Central Park—offers a peaceful retreat from the surrounding city. The park hosts numerous community events, festivals, races, and outdoor programs throughout the year, reinforcing its role as a central gathering space for Dallas.

A Place for Recreation, Nature, and Community

White Rock Lake Park offers a wide variety of active and passive recreational opportunities. The park’s scenic loop trail attracts thousands of visitors annually for walking, jogging, skating, and cycling, while its shoreline provides opportunities for fishing, kayaking, sailing, and wildlife observation. The lake’s natural areas support a diverse ecosystem, making it one of the best locations in Dallas for experiencing native plants, migratory birds, and local wildlife.

The park’s landscapes also provide spaces for relaxation and connection. Visitors enjoy picnicking beneath mature trees, watching sunsets over the water, exploring gardens, and gathering with friends and family. Popular destinations throughout the park include scenic overlooks, recreational fields, historic structures, and areas designed for community activities.

Beyond recreation, White Rock Lake has played an important role in preserving Dallas’ environmental and cultural heritage. Organizations and community groups have worked for decades to protect the lake’s natural beauty, improve amenities, and ensure that future generations can continue enjoying this urban treasure. Its combination of open space, history, and accessibility makes White Rock Lake Park a signature destination within Dallas’ park system.

White Rock Lake During World War II

During World War II, White Rock Lake became the site of a unique chapter in Dallas history when it served as a prisoner-of-war camp for captured German soldiers. In 1944, approximately 300 German prisoners were housed near Winfrey Point after the U.S. Army established a temporary camp at the lake. The prisoners lived in barracks originally built in the 1930s for the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC), a federal program created during the Great Depression to provide employment while improving public lands.

The CCC workers who lived at White Rock Lake helped build many of the park’s early amenities, including the Sunset Inn, picnic areas, the Big Thicket building, and other infrastructure that shaped the lake’s development. They also installed concrete bollards around the lake, many of which remain part of the park’s landscape today. The Winfrey Point building was also begun during this period before the United States entered World War II and the CCC program came to an end.

When the German POW camp opened, prisoners constructed security fencing and guard stations around the barracks. Each evening, the soldiers were transported by bus to Fair Park, where they worked in an Army quartermaster repair facility repairing uniforms, shoes, helmets, tents, and other military equipment. The work supported the war effort while providing prisoners with structured activities and wages paid through canteen coupons.

Unlike some other prisoner camps across Texas, no escapes were reported from the White Rock Lake facility. After the war ended in 1945, the prisoners were returned to Europe, and the Dallas Parks Department regained control of the park lake in 1946. The former barracks later served another purpose, housing military veterans attending Southern Methodist University through the G.I. Bill. Today, little remains of the POW camp except for a historic fire hydrant marking the location of this lesser-known chapter in the park’s past.

Preserving White Rock Lake’s Legacy

For more than a century, White Rock Lake Park has reflected the growth, challenges, and changing needs of Dallas. What began as a critical water source became a place of recreation, conservation, and community connection. Its rich history—from early water infrastructure and the work of the Civilian Conservation Corps to its role during World War II—adds depth to the natural beauty that draws millions of visitors.

As Dallas continues to grow, White Rock Lake remains a vital reminder of the importance of preserving public spaces. The park continues to serve as a place where people can connect with nature, celebrate community traditions, and experience a historic landscape that has shaped the city for generations.
