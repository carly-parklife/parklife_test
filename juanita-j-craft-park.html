A Legacy of Courage, Equality, and Community

Juanita J. Craft Park is a 21.5-acre community park with deep roots in the local Black community surrounding Wahoo Lake. Originally known as Wahoo Park, the property was purchased in 1924 and later renamed in honor of Juanita J. Craft, one of Dallas’s most influential civil rights leaders.

The park serves as a lasting tribute to Juanita J. Craft, whose decades of advocacy transformed Dallas and helped expand access to public spaces, education, and opportunities for all residents.

Known as the “first lady of Dallas civil rights,” Craft dedicated her life to fighting for equality. Her work helped ensure that parks, restaurants, theaters, and other public spaces could be enjoyed by everyone, regardless of race. Today, the park that bears her name reflects her lifelong commitment to inclusion, service, and community.

The landscape itself reinforces the park’s connection to North Texas. Mature native live oaks, cedar elms, and pecan trees dot the grounds, providing shade and anchoring the park in the region’s traditional tree canopy.

As Craft once said, “For the first half of my life, they wouldn’t let me in the parks. Now they’re naming one after me.”

Honoring a Civil Rights Trailblazer

Juanita J. Craft’s impact on Dallas history extends far beyond the boundaries of the park named in her honor. Born in 1902, Craft became a powerful advocate for civil rights after joining the NAACP in 1935. Over the course of her career, she became one of the most important leaders in the Dallas civil rights movement.

Craft worked tirelessly to make public facilities inclusive throughout the city, including parks, restaurants, theaters, and other places where people of color had been denied equal access. She also played a major role in integrating the State Fair of Texas, helping open one of Dallas’s most prominent public events to all residents.

Her leadership reached across the state as well. Craft led more than 180 NAACP chapters throughout Texas, inspiring communities to organize and advocate for change. Her South Dallas home became a gathering place for civil rights discussions and hosted notable leaders, including Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and President Lyndon B. Johnson.

Through decades of activism, Craft helped reshape Dallas into a more inclusive city and created opportunities that continue to benefit generations of residents.

A Park Dedicated to Community and Connection

Originally known as Wahoo Park, the property was purchased in 1924 and later renamed Juanita J. Craft Park in 1973 to honor the celebrated civil rights leader. The dedication formally connected Craft’s name and legacy to a neighborhood and public space deeply rooted in the surrounding community.

Today, Juanita J. Craft Park provides a welcoming space for recreation, connection, and neighborhood gatherings. The 21.5-acre park features courts, athletic fields, trails, and open spaces where residents can enjoy outdoor activities and spend time together.

Mature live oaks, cedar elms, and pecan trees contribute to the park’s established character, while its setting near Wahoo Lake connects the landscape to the area’s history and natural environment.

The park’s amenities reflect the values that defined Craft’s work: creating places where people can gather, build relationships, and participate fully in community life. Every game played, walk taken, and gathering held within the park continues the spirit of connection and opportunity that Craft championed throughout her life.

Growing a Legacy of Stewardship

The park’s connection to its natural setting continues to evolve. In October 2025, the Dallas Parks “Branching Out” program brought volunteers together to plant additional native trees at Juanita J. Craft Park, strengthening the park’s tree canopy and supporting its ecological health.

These new plantings complement the park’s mature native trees, including live oak, cedar elm, and pecan, while reinforcing the landscape’s connection to traditional North Texas species.

The ongoing investment in the park’s trees reflects a broader legacy of community stewardship. Just as Craft worked to expand access to public spaces for future generations, the care and planting of trees help ensure that those spaces remain healthy, welcoming, and resilient for years to come.

Continuing Juanita J. Craft’s Vision

Nearby, the Juanita J. Craft Recreation Center at 4500 Spring Avenue continues this legacy of service by providing additional resources and programming for residents of all ages. Recently renovated, the recreation center includes a Baylor Diabetes Wellness Clinic, a state-of-the-art fitness center, meeting spaces, and a full gymnasium.

The facility offers a wide range of programs designed to support the community, including after-school programs, camps, youth and adult sports, and fitness and wellness classes. Together, the park and recreation center create spaces where residents can learn, grow, and connect.

Juanita J. Craft Park stands as more than a recreational destination. Its history, mature trees, new native plantings, and connection to Wahoo Lake are woven together with the story of a woman who spent her life breaking barriers and ensuring that future generations could experience the opportunities she was once denied.

Her legacy lives on through every person who gathers at the park, participates in community programs, cares for its landscape, and enjoys the public spaces she fought to make accessible for all Dallas residents.
